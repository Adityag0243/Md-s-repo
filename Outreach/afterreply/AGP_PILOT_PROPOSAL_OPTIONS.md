# AGP pilot proposal: how to think about it

Working notes for our reply to David Tile's RFQ (Agency Growth Partners, Oct 2026).
Each section has three parts: **what they asked**, **what we say**, and **why we say it**.
The last sections have the questions to ask David and a draft one-page proposal.

---

## 1. The whole thing in simple words

**What AGP does:** AGP helps small marketing agencies get more clients. Most of these
agencies are run by the founder, who does the client work *and* all the selling. AGP takes
over the selling.

**What AGP wants from us:** AGP needs new agency clients itself. It wants people like us
to find agency founders and message them by email or LinkedIn. When a founder replies
"yes, interested", we pass that reply to AGP's sales team (they call them **SDRs**). The
SDRs book a call, and David closes the deal.

```
 We find agency founders → we message them → founder replies "interested"
        → we pass it to AGP's SDR team → SDR books the call → David closes
```

**What they asked for:** a one-page plan for a small test (a **pilot**), usually 30 days.

### Words used in this doc

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Positive reply** | The person replied and is interested ("sure, let's talk", "send more info") |
| **Booked appointment / meeting** | A call is actually on the calendar. **AGP's SDRs do this, not us** |
| **SDR** | AGP's sales person who takes our positive replies and books the call |
| **Segment** | Which type of agency we message |
| **Angle** | The one main point our message makes |
| **Lane** | Our own area (type of agency + country). AGP gives different lanes to different people so nobody messages the same person twice |
| **Inbox / sending domain** | The email account we send from, e.g. `aditya@something.com` |
| **Warmed inbox** | An email account that has been used slowly for a few weeks, so Gmail/Outlook trust it and don't send its mails to spam. A brand-new inbox can only send ~10–20 mails a day at first |

## 2. Is this a good lead?

**Yes, worth trying.** Think of it as a small paid test first, not a big contract.

**Why it's good:**
- **They need help urgently.** They want 100–150 meetings by Jan 1, which is only about 12 weeks away.
- **It can grow.** The RFQ says that once we deliver for AGP, there is more work inside
  their client agencies. Every agency AGP signs will also need outbound, so AGP could
  bring us more clients later.
- **Their method matches our product.** They want small, well-researched lists, not mass
  emails. That is exactly what ClawReach does.

**What to be careful about:**
- **We are not the only ones pitching.** Other people are sending proposals too.
- **Agency founders get a lot of "I'll get you leads" messages.** Generic messages won't
  work, so our message must be specific (section 4).
- **We met them only 1–2 days ago.** Don't ask for big things like their LinkedIn account
  or their email domains on day 1. Build trust first.
- **AGP is new.** Their website shows no client names or case studies yet. Take a part of
  the payment upfront.
- **Their website and the RFQ say different things:** the website says agencies at $1M–5M,
  the RFQ says $500K–3M. Ask which one they want.

## 4. Segment and angle (who we message, and what we say)

**What they asked:** "The segment and angle you'd run."

### Quick comparison

| Option | Who we contact | What we tell them (in one line) |
|---|---|---|
| **A** ⭐ | SEO / content agencies that started selling "AI search" services | "You have a new service. Nobody is selling it while you're busy doing client work." |
| **B** | Agencies that get their work from HubSpot / Shopify / Webflow referrals | "You depend on referrals. You need your own way to get clients." |
| **C** | Any marketing agency that is about to hire its first salesperson | "Before you hire a salesperson, talk to us." |
| **D** | Agencies whose clients are SaaS companies | "You get leads for your clients, but not for yourself." |

### Option A: SEO and content agencies that now sell "AI search" ⭐ recommended

**Background:** People now ask ChatGPT and Google AI for answers, so normal SEO is getting
weaker. Many SEO agencies have started a new service to get their clients to show up in AI
answers (called "AEO" or "GEO").

- **Who:** founders of SEO / content agencies (around 8–40 people) that added this new service recently.
- **What we say:** "You built a new service, but you're the only one selling it, and you're
  busy with client work. AGP can sell it for you."
- **How we find them:** their website has a new "AI search / AEO / GEO" page, or the
  founder posts about it on LinkedIn.
- **Why pick this:**
  - DoableClaw builds AI-search tools itself, so we understand this topic.
  - David ran an SEO / content agency, so he'll get the idea immediately.
  - AGP's own website lists "AI-optimization" agencies as a target.

### Option B: Agencies that live on platform referrals

**Background:** Many agencies are official partners of HubSpot, Shopify, Klaviyo or Webflow.
These platforms send them clients, and they publish partner lists with levels (Gold,
Platinum…). A higher level usually means a bigger agency.

- **Who:** founders of agencies in these partner lists.
- **What we say:** "Most of your clients come from referrals. If those stop, your
  pipeline stops. AGP builds you your own."
- **Why pick this:** the lists are public and real, so fewer emails bounce and AGP's email
  reputation stays safe.

### Option C: Agencies about to hire their first salesperson

**Background:** When an agency posts a job for its first salesperson, it means the
founder is tired of selling alone. That is exactly the problem AGP solves.

- **Who:** any marketing agency showing this.
- **What we say:** "Hiring a salesperson is slow and risky. AGP can run the whole sales
  process for you instead."
- **How we find them:** a sales job post, "book a call with the founder" as the only
  contact option on the website, a growing team with nobody in sales, or the founder
  posting about doing all the sales.
- **Why pick this:** finding these signals is what ClawReach is built for. Someone who
  just buys a list can't do it.

### Option D: Agencies whose clients are SaaS companies

- **Who:** marketing agencies that mostly work for software companies.
- **What we say:** "You get leads for your SaaS clients every day, but your own new
  clients still come only from referrals."
- **Why pick this:** our best LinkedIn results so far were with B2B SaaS people (section 8).

### Which country?

AGP says lanes can't share contacts, so we need our own area. Let **David decide**, since
he knows which lanes are already taken. Our preference:
- **UK, Ireland, Australia / New Zealand:** probably fewer others targeting them. Our India
  working hours match UK mornings and Australia afternoons, so we can reply fast.
- **USA:** the biggest market, but others are most likely targeting it too.

**Our suggestion:** Option A in the UK / Australia, using Option C's signals to decide who
to message first.

## 5. What we promise in the first month

**What they asked:** "Appointments you expect to book in the first 30 days."

**What we say:** we promise **positive replies**, not booked meetings.

| Week | What happens | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Setup: pick the lane with David, build the list, research each agency, write the messages, get David's OK | No replies yet. Nothing is sent before the list and messages are ready |
| **Week 2** | Start sending | **5–10 positive replies** |
| **Week 3** | Improve based on what worked in week 2 | **~10 positive replies** |
| **Week 4** | Keep it steady | **~10 positive replies** |
| **Month 1 total** | | **~20 positive replies** (if it goes well, 25–30) |

Then we explain what that could mean in meetings, **without promising it**:
"If your SDRs book about a third to half of these, that is roughly 6–10 meetings in month 1."

**Why we promise replies and not meetings:**
1. **Replies are in our control. Meetings are not.** AGP's SDRs book the calls. If they
   are slow, meetings drop even if our work is good.
2. **We don't know the numbers yet.** We have never messaged agency founders. Promising
   meetings now would be a guess. Promising replies is based on real numbers we have seen.
3. **Week 1 is setup on purpose.** AGP's rules say "verified lists, warmed inboxes, no
   blasts". Rushing on day 1 would break their own rules.
4. **A small promise we beat is better than a big promise we miss.** It builds trust,
   and trust gets us the bigger deal later.

**Why ~10 replies a week is realistic:** our past campaigns got interested replies from
about 6–9% of the people we contacted (section 8). Agency founders get more cold messages,
so assume about half that, **~3–4%**. To get 10 interested replies a week, we need to
contact about **250–300 new people a week**. Section 6 shows we can do that.

## 6. Channels and weekly volume

**What they asked:** "Channels and weekly volume."

**What we say:** "Email + LinkedIn, from our own accounts, building up week by week."

| | Week 1 | Week 2 | Weeks 3–4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email (our warmed inboxes) | 0 (setup) | ~150–250 emails | ~400–500 emails/week |
| LinkedIn (our own accounts) | 0 (setup) | ~50–100 connection requests | ~100–150 requests/week |
| New people contacted | 0 | ~150 | ~250–300 |

(One person gets about 2–3 emails over 2 weeks: a first email and 1–2 follow-ups. That is
why emails sent is higher than people contacted.)

**Why our own accounts:**
- We already have warmed inboxes and several LinkedIn accounts (including Sales
  Navigator) connected to our system. So we can start in week 2 without waiting on AGP.
- Asking for David's LinkedIn or AGP's email domains right after meeting him is too much.
  We earn that later.

**The one catch: the sender's name.** The email will come from *our* domain but talk about
AGP. Two ways to handle it (ask David which he prefers):
1. We write as ourselves, "working with Agency Growth Partners".
2. Later, if the pilot goes well, AGP gives us 2–3 inboxes on an AGP domain, so emails come
   from AGP directly.

**Why not more volume?** We *can* send more (500+ a week). But AGP clearly said "no blasts",
and agency founders hate mass emails. Small and researched fits what they asked for.

## 9. Pricing

**What they asked:** "Your pricing or comp model for the pilot."

**What we say:** a small, fixed fee for the 30-day pilot. After 30 days we fix the real
price together, based on the real numbers.

**Why this way:**
- Right now **nobody knows the real numbers**: how many replies become meetings, and how
  many meetings become AGP clients. Any price per meeting today would be a guess.
- After 30 days we'll know them, and can price fairly (per meeting, per client, or a monthly fee).
- **Why not do it for free?** A small fee makes both sides take it seriously (fast
  approvals, fast SDR follow-up), and it covers our data and tool costs.
- AGP is new, so ask for the fee **upfront**, or at least half upfront.

**Options for the pilot fee** (your call):

| Option | What it looks like | Good / bad |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Small fixed fee** ⭐ | One fixed amount for the month (e.g. **$500–1,000**), mainly covering data and tool costs | Simple and easy to say yes to |
| **2. Fee per interested reply** | A small amount for each interested reply we hand over | Fair, but harder to track and may cause arguments about what counts as "interested" |
| **3. Free pilot** | No fee for 30 days | Easiest to get, but it looks less serious and we carry all the cost |

**Line for the proposal:** "Pilot: a fixed $X for 30 days. After 30 days we'll look at the
real reply → meeting → client numbers together and agree a price, per meeting or per client,
that works for both of us."

## 10. Questions to ask David

Better to ask than to guess. Send these with the proposal, or ask on WhatsApp first.

1. **Lane:** Which type of agency and which countries are free? Is "SEO / content agencies
   in the UK / Australia" OK?
2. **Size:** Your website says $1M–5M and the RFQ says $500K–3M. Which one should we target?
3. **Sending:** Is it OK if we send from our own accounts and say we're working with AGP?
   Or do you want emails to come from an AGP domain later?
4. **Meetings:** When does a meeting count, when it's booked or when it actually happens?
   Who decides if a lead is "qualified"?
5. **Handoff:** Who on your SDR team should get our positive replies, and how fast do they
   usually respond?
6. **Messaging:** Do you have a short description of AGP's offer we should use? Anything
   we must not say?
7. **Pilot length:** Is 30 days what you have in mind, and when would you like to start?
