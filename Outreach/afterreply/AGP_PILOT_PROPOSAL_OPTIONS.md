## 1. Is this a good lead?

**Yes, worth pitching. Treat it as a paid pilot that can turn into a channel, not a
big contract.**

For it:
- A real founder with a hard deadline: 100 to 150 appointments by Jan 1 is about **12 weeks**,
  so they need roughly **9 to 13 meetings a week** across all GTM engineers. They need people now.
- They already pay for performance (commission + equity), so a performance-based
  comp model fits how they think.
- The real prize is in this line of the RFQ: *"GTM opportunities inside our client work too …
  once you've delivered for AGP."* Every agency AGP signs needs outbound too, so AGP
  could become a reseller/channel for ClawReach.
- Their method (signal-led, tight lists, no blasts) is exactly what ClawReach does.

Watch-outs:
- **It's an RFQ.** Several GTM engineers are pitching, each in a separate lane. Expect
  comparison on price and on the "appointments in 30 days" number.
- **Agency founders are the most cold-pitched people on earth.** Generic "I'll get you
  leads" mail dies. The angle has to be specific (see §2).
- **We don't book the calls.** We hand positive replies to their SDRs. So "appointments
  booked" partly depends on their team. Define the metric carefully (§5).
- **Payment risk** with a new US company. Ask for the base fee upfront or 50/50 and invoice
  the per-meeting fees weekly.
- **Range mismatch:** $500K–3M in the RFQ vs $1M–5M on the site. Ask which one wins.

## 2. Segment and angle (pick 1 main + maybe 1 backup)

Lanes can't share contacts, so frame each lane as **segment × geography** and
ask David which lanes are already taken.

### Option A: SEO/content agencies moving into AI search (AEO/GEO) ⭐ recommended
- **Who:** SEO and content agencies, 8 to 40 staff, that have added an "AI search / GEO / AEO /
  LLM visibility" service in the last 6 months.
- **Angle:** "You rebuilt the offer for AI search. Who's selling it while you deliver?"
  They have a new productised offer (which AGP's playbook needs) but no one to sell it.
- **Signals:** new AEO/GEO service page, founder posting about AI Overviews or traffic
  drops, new pricing page, job posts for delivery roles but none for sales.
- **Why us:** DoableClaw builds AI-visibility/AEO tooling ourselves, so we know this market's
  language better than a generic SDR shop does. It also fits David (content/SEO background) and
  AGP's own "AI-optimization" category.

### Option B: Platform-partner agencies (HubSpot, Shopify/Klaviyo, Webflow, Google Partners)
- **Who:** agencies listed in partner directories. The tier (Gold/Platinum/Diamond, Shopify
  Plus partner) is a decent revenue proxy, and the lists come pre-verified.
- **Angle:** "Partner referrals are a channel you don't control." Their pipeline depends on
  the platform sending them work, and the founder closes it all.
- **Signals:** tier changes, new partner badges, new case studies, hiring.
- **Why us:** clean, verifiable list source, which protects AGP's domains. D2C/e-commerce
  research is something we've already done (Shopify/Klaviyo shops serve D2C).

### Option C: "The founder is still the closer" (signal-led, any marketing agency)
- **Who:** agencies showing they're about to hire their first salesperson, or that badly need one.
- **Angle:** "Before you hire your first BDR or sales head, talk to us." AGP replaces
  that hire.
- **Signals:** job posts for BDR/AE/Head of Growth/Partnerships, "book a call with [founder]"
  as the only sales path on the site, founder posting about sales burnout or feast-and-famine,
  delivery headcount rising with zero sales titles on LinkedIn.
- **Why us:** this is ClawReach's Signal Library in action (People & Hiring, Pain & Voice,
  Growth & Launches groups). Hardest to copy for a list-buying competitor.
- **Note:** this lane cuts across segments, so pin it to one geography to stay clean.

### Option D: B2B SaaS-focused demand-gen / PPC agencies
- **Who:** agencies whose clients are SaaS companies (paid, demand gen, SaaS SEO).
- **Angle:** "You build pipeline for SaaS companies. Your own pipeline is referrals."
- **Why us:** our best recent numbers are LinkedIn into **B2B SaaS** (see §6), so we know
  how these people respond.

### Geography add-on (combine with A, B, C or D)
- **UK + Ireland + Australia/NZ (+ Canada):** probably less crowded than the US by other
  GTM engineers. Our IST working hours cover UK mornings and AU afternoons live, so
  replies get handled fast.
- **US only:** biggest pool, but most likely to clash with other lanes.
- Compliance: UK/EU B2B cold email is fine with legitimate interest + an easy opt-out.
  **Canada (CASL) is stricter**, so only use published business emails there.

### Wildcard (mention only if David likes it)
- **Indian/offshore marketing agencies selling to US/UK clients** ($500K–3M). Founder-led,
  no sales team, and our home turf. Risk: they may be off-ICP for AGP's pricing. Ask first.

**Suggested pick:** Option A in UK/IE/ANZ (or the US if those regions are taken), using
Option C's hiring/sales signals as the "why now" layer.

## 3. Channels and weekly volume

Everything depends on **whose inboxes and LinkedIn we use**. The RFQ says "protect the
sending domains … warmed inboxes", which strongly suggests **AGP has its own domains/inboxes
for GTM engineers**. Ask. Three scenarios to put in the proposal:

| Scenario | Email per week | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. AGP gives us warmed inboxes** (best: sender brand = AGP) | 3 inboxes → **~150 in wk 1**, 250 in wk 2, **400–500 by wk 3–4** (≈30–35/inbox/day cap) | Fastest and cleanest. With 5 inboxes we can reach ~600–750/wk |
| **2. New AGP look-alike domains** (e.g. `getagp.co`) we set up | Wk 1–2: warm-up only, ~50/inbox/wk; full volume from wk 3 | LinkedIn carries weeks 1–2. Domain cost ~$15 each + inbox ~$5–7/mo |
| **3. Our own warmed inboxes** | **200+ in wk 1, up to 500 if needed** | Ready now, but **the sender domain won't match AGP**. Use only as a 2-week bridge while scenario 2 warms up, if David agrees |

**LinkedIn:** whose account?
- **David's personal profile** (best: founder to founder, and his own blog says this matters)
  via our LinkedIn tool (Unipile). Safe pace is **~100–150 connection requests/week** plus
  follow-up DMs.
- Or an AGP SDR profile, or one of ours (weaker: a stranger pitching someone else's company).

**Suggested line for the proposal:** "Email + LinkedIn. 150 researched emails in week 1,
ramping to 400–500/week by week 3, plus 100–150 LinkedIn connection requests/week from
[David's / an AGP] profile. Every contact gets its own researched first line. No blasts."

## 4. Appointments in the first 30 days

⚠️ **"2 per month" is too low.** They need ~9 to 13 meetings a week overall. Even split across
5 engineers, that's 2 or 3 **per week** per lane. A 2-per-month figure will read as
"not serious" next to other pitches. Here is the maths, using our own past rates and
assuming agency founders reply at half the rate exporters did:

| | Contacts in 30 days | Meetings per contact | Meetings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email (3-step sequence, ~1,200–1,400 sends) | ~400–450 companies | 1.5–2% (we saw ~5% on leather exporters) | **6–9** |
| LinkedIn | ~350–450 requests | ~1% (we saw ~2% on B2B SaaS) | **3–5** |
| Minus week 1 (setup + warm-up, nothing lands yet) | | | **−30%** |

**Options to state:**
- **Safe:** "4–6 booked appointments in the first 30 days, 3–5 per week from month 2."
- **Recommended:** "**6–10** booked appointments in the first 30 days (we commit to 6),
  plus 15–25 positive replies handed to your SDRs."
- **Aggressive:** "10–12 in 30 days." Only if AGP gives warmed inboxes + David's LinkedIn on day 1.

Also say clearly: **week 1 is setup** (list, research, copy approved, inboxes checked), and
meetings start landing in **week 2**.

## 5. Pricing / comp model (pick one)

Rough US market for B2B appointment setting: **$150–500 per qualified meeting**, or
**$3–6K/month retainers**. We're India-based, so we can come in under that and
still make money. **Check these numbers before sending.**

| Option | Structure | Good for | Risk to us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Pay per meeting only** | $0 base, **$250 per held qualified meeting** | Winning the RFQ: zero risk for AGP | High: we eat data/tool costs if their SDRs book slowly |
| **B. Small base + per meeting** ⭐ | **$1,000/month base** (data, enrichment, research, LinkedIn tool) + **$150 per held qualified meeting** | Balanced; the base covers our hard costs | Low |
| **C. Base + meeting + close bonus** ⭐ | $750/month + $100/held meeting + **$1,000 per signed AGP client** from our lane | Matches their 11-client goal and their commission/equity mindset | Low–medium |
| **D. Flat pilot fee** | $2,000 for 30 days. If we land under 6 meetings, month 2 is free | Simple; shows confidence | Medium |
| **E. Per positive reply** | $75–100 per qualified positive reply handed over | Fair, because **their SDRs book the calls, not us** | Low; but they may prefer paying per meeting |

Things to define whatever we pick:
- **Qualified** = matches the ICP (founder-led marketing agency, $500K–3M, English-speaking) and
  the founder/CEO attends.
- **Held**, not just booked: they will ask for this. Counter with a short SDR-response
  window (e.g. the SDR replies to our handoff within 4 working hours), otherwise slow
  booking costs us.
- **Who pays for inboxes/domains/LinkedIn seats.** If ours, it's in the base.
- After the pilot: same terms month to month, or move to a bigger lane.

**Suggested:** B for a clean pitch, or C if you want to show we're aligned with their
outcome (David likes partner-style deals).

  time for positive replies.

- **Definition of a qualified meeting** in writing.
- **Data budget:** we cover our own data/enrichment in the base fee (simplest), *or* they
  provide Apollo/Clay credits.
